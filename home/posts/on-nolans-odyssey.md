I wasn't looking forward to seeing this movie but as someone who grew
up on Greek mythology, I suppose I had to. As expected, I was
extremely underwhelmed and couldn't wait for it to finish. Now that
the dust (and hype) has settled, I came back to thinking about *why* I
disliked it so much. Nolan is obviously a great filmmaker, and this is
a massively hyped and loved movie, isn't it? Anyway, I thought about
it and I think I can articulate what I think is going on:

*Nolan. Doesn't. Like. The. Odyssey.*

I don't have any other explanation for the bizarre choices he made all
through the movie.

Why else would he take a fun, vibrant adventure about a wily,
charismatic character facing off against one monstrous challenge after
the other and turn it into a drab, colourless, joyless story of a
soldier's PTSD from killing too many people?

Why else would he impose his own shallow morality and world-view on a
story told a few thousand years back, when *any* student of history
will tell you that the first lesson of reading historical fiction is
to avoid
[presentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentism_(historical_analysis)).
Note for example how the movie's Odysseus simply cannot be shown to
even partially enjoy his captivity under Calypso. Oh no, Calypso has
to be spiking his food and causing amnesia, because our white
monogamous audience would be too confused by his behaviour.

Why else would he take powerful women (like the enigmatic and
dangerous Circe, or wise Athena) and reduce them to trauma-shocked
victims of male violence?

Why else would he remove all the Gods from the story, without which
context is missing: Poseidon vows to make Odysseus' return a misery
beacuse of what he did to the Cyclops Polyphemus; Athena however is
sympathetic to his plight. Not to mention that incidents like the 
one with Apollo's sheep simply don't make sense without those Gods
standing by ready to make good on their curse.

Why else would he constantly harp about unrelated asides (the Sea
People; Zeus' Law) and try to shoehorn them into a forced 'twist' that
he apparently is compelled to use in all his movies? Hey guess what,
the Greeks were *invading* Troy and only a wild, desperate stretch
would compare that to being their *guests*. So, you could hardly say
they were guilty of breaking any laws of hospitality by tricking them
with a gift horse. By any definition, it was the Trojans who were
guests of the Greeks and guilty of breaching Zeus's law when Paris
eloped with Helen. But that wouldn't fit the narrative would it, we
can't show any complexity or the audience would get confused.

I am all for each
new generation rewriting myths of old (see the Coen Brothers' *O
Brother, Where Art Thou?* for a better interpretation of the Odyssey,
or Dan Simmons' masterful sci-fi rewrite of the Greek mythologies
in *Ilium*), but this one just left me a bit tired.

NOLAN

Cast: *Christopher Nolan*, now in his twilight years. His muse,
*Mnemosyne*, who may or may not exist and we only hear her voice (We
need to keep the audience debating don't we).

Nolan in his old age, weathered and tired. Dull, grey colours.

MNEMOSYNE: "You were great once. What happened?"

OLD NOLAN (defiant): "I'm still great. It's them... They don't get me
anymore. I gave them legends. See how they treat me."

*Flashback to Nolan in his youth, filming Memento. Colours are
vibrant. We see Nolan enthusiastically creating meta-narratives,
looping stories, and not talking down to his audience.*

MNEMOSYNE (voice over): "Where is that Nolan now?"

OLD NOLAN (defiant): "I had my fill with those stories. What
captivates me more is guilt. The guilt of Oppenheimer. The guilt of
Odysseus."

MNEMOSYNE: "Guilt? Odysseus had no guilt. He had regret. He cried for
the years he lost. He accepted his fate and waged a war he could not
escape from, and paid for it with an arduous journey back."

OLD NOLAN (doubtful): "But the guilt.. the guilt.."

*Cut to close up of Nolan's withered face. His famous twist is about
to come.*

MNEMOSYNE: "The guilt was in you."
